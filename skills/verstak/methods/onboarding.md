# onboarding — from no working graph to working on one's own

**Use when:** the person has **no working graph** — readiness and `verstak_realm(action="list")` establish only their personal mind, or only graphs owned by others or an organization while no `AGENTS.md` names one; or, in a git repository without our cover in `AGENTS.md` (a start hook alone is none), the person asks to set it up, whatever graphs they have — never offered there unasked (the door, `Start`); it is offered by itself only in a session the door's `Start` runs in, from the door's Survey or from `methods/align.md`, without a separate call, and runs on the person's yes — the request they came with doesn't wait for it. Or the person asks: an explicit "onboarding" or a redo runs whatever the signs; only words like "help me get started", "I don't know where to start" are gated by them — **while a lasting sign below is missing**.

**Not when:** every lasting sign below is met and the person says they don't know where to start. Decide by the lasting signs — a working graph, the person's role in it, and in a harness the repository's holon and agent role; a missing seat or hooks alone routes to Start or align, never to onboarding. They have a graph and know it; the words are about their task. Ask what they are working on now and help on the material that exists (`methods/entry.md`, then the method the task needs). Explaining the graph to someone who has kept it for months is the failure here.

**Grounding:** accompanying a step includes a meaningful move **and telling the person its result and what it means**, in a form that invites the next move. A structural artifact counts when it exists with the required ownership or binding; explaining it is the agent's obligation, not an additional progress gate. Separate knowledge steps may need the current person's confirmation through the readiness API. Every accompanied step ends in a sentence to the person, not in a write.

One loop serves every entry: **read the signs → choose one step → do it with the person → say the result and its meaning → read again**. The table drives it, not a fixed sequence of screens; the harness branch below fixes only the order of its first four steps.

## Signs, read from data

Read for **this person** and **the chosen graph**: the account is the person's; roles, repositories and subject nodes belong to one graph. A role in another graph does not count here; personal mind is never a working graph; a graph the person can read is not yet a graph they chose.

Read the missing signs with `verstak_me(action="readiness", realm)` — each sign with its state, receipt and next step, the artifacts' links; without `realm` the person's signs, with it that graph's. The table's other reads can guide subject work; they do not turn an API `unknown` into a passed or missing readiness sign. Readiness is `complete` and the signs with their `ready` (the API's `items`), retold from the answer, never composed; the count `progress: N/M` in the answer's header counts the `nudges`, not steps: never say it as the person's progress ("progress: 5 of 9" is the misreading) — say which signs are missing and which the answer could not establish, and name the nudges without a number, as what they can also do. Where the runtime posts your turn's answer into the case itself (the conversation home's onboarding case), don't call `verstak_case(action="say")` there. Structural signs need existence and the appropriate ownership or user binding, not an additional receipt. The service names and values below — `properties`/`attrs`, `identity`, `complete=false`, `unknown`/`source_unavailable`, `persona` — are never retold to the person; say what they mean in the person's words.

Only a separate knowledge step needing confirmation uses `verstak_me(action="understood", key=<key printed by readiness>)`, after the person's own move — their answer or action about this step's subject — and the result's meaning have been shown; a yes to your explanation, a confirmation of identity or an answer to another question is no move of the step. First read `verstak_me(action="?")`: the contract is `understood(key, realm?, subject?, values?)`. `realm` names the step's graph; `subject` is the existing node the person created or chose and was shown; `values` are values the person confirmed. The platform's refusal says which fields that key requires. Supply only the requested fields from established data and retry; missing data means ask the person, not guess or create another artifact. Success prints a receipt; a repeat returns that same receipt. A failed confirmation stays a failure; reread readiness and never replace it with a marker.

**No onboarding-only markers in properties/attrs:** read readiness from current subject data and the platform's receipts outside attributes. Never read or write profile, graph or node markers to assert progress, and never persist a pointer to mind. Subject attributes used outside onboarding, such as a holon's `repository` binding, are valid sources of those facts, including for readiness; they are not personal completion flags. Existing artifacts and evidence of understanding are separate; an agent's own write proves no human understanding.

**Each person's progress is their own.** A shared graph's artifacts may be reused, but another person's role or receipt does not count for the current person. Check their role through its `user` binding in the chosen graph; never by author, name or attributes. Read and confirm only the current identity's progress; another member's onboarding history is not exposed or retold. The companion's runtime `persona` is not an onboarding predicate. A surface still requiring it or a receipt over a structural fact is a platform mismatch: report it, do not manufacture a marker or claim the step passed.

A missing public handle leaves `identity` incomplete and overall `complete=false`; only the UI shows that reminder and unfinished progress. This method neither asks for a handle nor delays ordinary work because of those values, and never declares overall completion against the API's answer.

**A failed read is "not established", never "absent".** An error, a refusal or a silent tool is not an empty list: restore access (`methods/establish-mcp.md`) and read again; never create a graph, role or holon because a read failed. This method reads neither Telegram nor the plan: they are never reported missing, only explained in step 6.

Repository attributes in the table locate the specific repository's holon and are also a valid subject source for readiness. Read the value, not just the key: absent, empty or null is no binding; repeated copies of one address are not several repositories. Shared bindings may count for participants who can access them without revealing anyone else's completion history. Readiness's state still comes from the API; one matching holon does not declare every sign met in `Not when`.

If readiness cannot establish a repository or agent role from an adequate source, keep `unknown` / `source_unavailable`; storage in attrs alone is no reason for unknown. Agent-role evidence must establish the role in the required scope through its operational links or attributes; an arbitrary unbound `adhikarin` or another person's standing is insufficient. That API uncertainty alone neither authorizes creating a replacement nor forbids subject work: step 4 may reuse or create on the person's choice and a successful search establishing what is absent. The API sign remains unknown until its source establishes it.

## Choose the next step

1. Read the table from the top; the first missing sign is the candidate. In the harness branch the order below wins.
2. **Offer one step**, in English (the door, `Communication`): what you will do, what it leaves behind, what it is for, and the condition that matters (who will own it, whether it can be renamed). One step, not the tour.
3. The person goes on, refines or defers. A refinement changes the step, not the order; a deferral ("skip") leads on to the next missing sign (`Deferral and redo`).

**Steps are offers, not conditions.** While onboarding is unfinished, everything the person can do in their position stays open: reading and searching the graph, talking to the assistant, writing where they have the right. A missing step — their role, the first holon, a repository, the bot, a harness — is offered as a hint, a line, a word, never made a condition of showing, input or conversation; only what is impossible by nature without it waits: a write without the right to write, a seat without a role.

Already present → reused, shown with its current value, never created again. The person who still doesn't understand gets the explanation again on what exists, not a second copy of the artifact.

**What exists is not remade.** The names and content of the person's graph, holons and roles are neither renamed nor rewritten without their word (binding the repository: step 4); where they diverge from the area, name the divergence and ask. No umbrella holon over existing ones for the sake of onboarding. The `AGENTS.md` cover's fields and `Project` slots are align's ordinary update.

## Accompany the chosen step

1. **Do it with the person** — their words for names, their choice where there is one, asked in words, never through the harness's option picker: it puts an answer in place of the question; search before every create.
2. **Say the result and what it means**, explicitly: "Created the graph *X* — your place of work; what we learn about this area goes there." A step that ended in a refusal or an unmet condition says what did not happen and why — no confirmation the data doesn't show. Structural existence is already the structural sign; only a knowledge step needing a receipt uses `understood` after the person shows they understood (`Signs, read from data`).
3. Nodes you write on the person's word are written by you, never as the person. That you wrote the node doesn't make the step theirs — their move and the result you told them do.
4. **Read again** before the next choice: the reads above, not the previous answer. Switched graphs → every graph-scope sign is read afresh there.

## 0 · Personal memory — in every branch

In the home branch, readiness's `personal_mind` sign decides alone: present → one line in the word to the person, no further read; missing → the provision below. In the harness branch, and in the home when it is missing, load `methods/minding.md` and follow its **Personal memory before reading or writing** entry, then its personal-graph provision for the established gaps. This is the single path: reuse what exists; a failed read is not absence. The personal graph's address is always `mind`; never ask for a handle, build a full address or save a memory pointer.

In the harness branch, then read readiness again (in the home, the one line goes into the opening's word). Tell the person exactly what exists and what remains unestablished: personal memory lets the assistant remember and recall what is theirs across projects — their machines, people, habits, their own environment. Work never goes there: a project's deployment, infrastructure and decisions go to that project's graph. A refused API operation or a missing server capability is named directly, never repaired with a progress attribute.

Filling mind — areas, what each live graph is for, people, their machines and local setup — is not an onboarding step: only on the person's request (`methods/minding.md`). Personal mind with the current person's bound role is the minimal structural sign, not filled memory; a runtime assistant role is not an extra onboarding requirement. Artifacts present and the person understanding them are checked apart.

## The home branch — a window or bot thread

**The person sees every call; words come before reads.** The thread shows each tool call to the person, and a page of calls before the first sentence reads as a hang. The opening is fixed; step 0 opens every home thread, whatever its first word (the phrase or a readiness key) — unless the window already showed it, below — and steps 1–4 run once a graph is chosen:
0. **The opening line, first and before any call** — unless the window already showed it (the person came from the window's wizard: name and handle filled, the window's template gives the opening and the closing; don't repeat them) — this text, polished in wording only, never in meaning or place: "This is Verstak. It organizes memory, thinking, planning, and collaboration between agents. To get started, let's write down who you are — and tell me what you'd like to work on."
1. One read — `verstak_me(action="readiness", realm)`. The opening doesn't wait for an answer: this read, the role and the question follow in the same turn; an answer to the opening that already says what the space is about and who they are is the question's answer.
2. **The person's role, unasked.** Readiness shows one bound to them → it is theirs, as it stands. None → one search for a role made for them in advance, `verstak_search(realm, q="", node_type="karta", manifested_as="svatantra", limit=50)`, paged while it reports more, and read the hits: an unbound one whose name is their name fits and is bound silently, `verstak_update(realm, node_id=<role>, user="me", basis_version=…)` — a role made for an invitation is bound by the invitation itself, never by a guess; none fits → create it at once — `verstak_add_karta(realm, name=<their name as readiness prints it>, motivation="<their name>'s place in this graph", manifested_as="svatantra", user="me")`, modes per `methods/writing.md`. No question first: it is their place in the graph, and they correct it later. This replaces step 3's explanation and proposal in the home.
3. **One question**, in English: "What is this space about, and who are you here?" — with one line on what now exists: this graph is theirs to work in, and they have a role in it.
4. **On the answer:** rename the role or rewrite its motivation if they want it, then go on in human terms with what they came for. The person knows nothing yet of agents, holons or repositories and need not: none is offered or explained before the closing, beyond the opening line's own words.

Never walk graphs to see what is in them — an empty graph is empty, readiness already says what is missing; no `verstak_admin` here (access, members), no listing of other graphs unless a graph is still to be chosen (step 1). A thread where step 1 already asked what the work is about takes that answer as the one question's answer. Each step after the opening takes at most three calls before you speak again.

**Plain words, nothing of the kitchen.** The person reads English sentences about their work: a graph, their role, an area of work. Never the method's vocabulary — `svatantra`, `adhikarin` or their glyphs, holon, contour, "root area", steward, batch, align, `rN`, `user=me`, a binding — and never the runtime's internals (seats, windows, what the thread could not take), tool names or raw answers — calls are not retold to the person. Questions are ones a person asks a person.

**Minimal steps, minimal load.** One message — one thought, and at most one question or one action; short sentences, at most three; no lists of options, no step-by-step plans, no explanations of how things work inside. The next step comes only after the person's answer. The opening line and the closing are the only messages longer than that.

**The closing ends onboarding explicitly**, once — unless the window's wizard already showed it — when the role is settled and the person's first piece of work is in the graph — or when they defer or move on to just talking — this text, polished in wording only: "From here we can keep talking, and I'll organize what you know about your project into the graph, recall it, and check it for consistency. To get the most out of Verstak, we recommend creating repositories for your project (several is better) and connecting agents with stronger models to work on it." It replaces the word about capabilities (6) in the home; billing is not said there unasked — on the person's question, from step 6's billing paragraph.

In the Verstak window's or the bot's onboarding thread there is no repository and no harness, and the thread may start with no graph chosen — then the person's own rows are read and **1 · Area and graph** chooses one. The repository check, steps 2 and 5 below, step 4's repository half (align, the `repository` attr, the agent role, `AGENTS.md`), and the table's rows for hooks, the agent role and the agent's seat do not run there; step 0 does. The loop goes the opening line → **0 · Personal memory** → **1 · Area and graph** when none is chosen → the person's role and the one question → the person's own work in human terms → the closing, which comes once when these are done and again only on an explicit redo — never every turn. The thread's first word, from the window's or the bot's button, is one of exactly two cases: a readiness sign's key — one Latin word, one of the item keys `verstak_me(action="readiness")` prints, in the form its `next.key` takes (e.g. `working_graph`) → after the opening line, lead from that step, a key of a step the home doesn't offer (`first_holon`, `work_area`, the repository's and agent's signs) → the opening; anything else — the phrase ("Start onboarding", or the same in the person's language; the bot always sends the phrase) → run the loop from the start.

**No holon is offered in the home.** When the person's own work needs a boundary in the graph, search for one that covers it (`verstak_search(realm, q=<the area>, node_type="holon")`) and reuse it, or create it on their word with `verstak_add_holon` — no `repository` attr — named in their words and spoken of as their area of work, never as a holon. Repositories and agents come only as the closing's offer — never before it; a repository is bound later by the agent in that repository — the harness branch — which finds the graph, role and area made here and reuses them.

You write under the companion's own seat, which the runtime gives it; the method takes no seat itself here. In a graph where the person's role is not yet bound, the thread has no seat: it reads and searches, and writes only the person's own role, the opening's step 2; every other write waits for the person's confirmation. Nodes written on the person's word keep the agent's authorship; the person's authorship is never faked.

## The harness branch — no working graph

It also runs in a repository without our cover, in any harness — only on the person's word: no start hook or plugin prompt says anything there, and nothing offers the branch unasked; their request doesn't wait for it. Our cover present with the rest missing is align's, not this branch's. A cover with another delivery's block or stamp and none of ours is a sign too: a sibling delivery aligned that repository, the start hook stays silent there, and neither this branch nor align runs unasked (the door, `Alignment`).

The order is fixed: personal memory (step 0) → graph → initial rituals → the person's role → the standard align move → the agent's seat → the word about capabilities.

In a graph with no agent role yet (steps 1–3) you write without a seat: there is no role of yours to stand on, and the person's role is never one. The first seat is the agent role's, once align creates it (step 4); from then on the door's seat rule holds.

**Which repository.** Before step 1 creates anything: `git remote get-url origin` and the graphs found. Show the person both and ask whether this repository is the work they want a graph for — its own question, not part of another; an answer to something else doesn't settle it: ask again until they answer, refuse or defer. Signs it may not be: an `upstream` remote (a fork); a tutorial, a client's or a sample repo by its README. No `origin` remote → ask which repository this is; the repository steps wait for the answer. A graph already listing this repository (the repositories read above, run on each listed graph) is the likely answer — show it and ask, as for any graph; several → ask which. Not this repository → no hooks, holon or `AGENTS.md` here; a graph for their area is still offered (step 1), and the repository steps wait for the repository they name. No person in the session (an autonomous start) → create nothing (the mind graph excepted); the greeting says onboarding is waiting for them.

**The person already has working graphs** (the branch ran on an unaligned repository) → one question before any first move: connect this repository to one of them (list them; `origin` and a holon whose `repository` attr names it are the hint) or start a new one — even one graph named like the repository is asked, never taken. No answer, no graph chosen or created, and no holon, role, case or file.

**1 · Area and graph.** Ask what they will work on — the area, in their words, before graph and repository. Then:
- graphs owned by others or their organizations are listed → name them ("*X* is shared with you"; in a harness also "and already holds repositories *A*, *B*"); continuing there is the default offered, never taken without their answer; a graph of their own the exception they choose;
- none → offer `verstak_realm(action="create", name=<the area in their words>)`, named for the area rather than the repository.

Say what a graph is for them: their place of work (in a harness: shared with their agents), the same in the window and the bot.

**2 · Hooks — the initial rituals.** Right after the graph, before the person's role: they need only the chosen graph, readable. Run `methods/align.md`'s bridge step now with that graph alone (Round 1, step 4: `prepare --json --graph rN`): it writes the cover with the graph, the memory guard beside others' entries and, where missing, the `AGENTS.md` skeleton, the `REALITY.md` stub and the `CLAUDE.md` pointer. From that cover the delivery speaks the push and merge reminders and, at start, the onboarding's early word, so the next start in this repository reads that graph and continues from the first missing sign; the full start word follows once the cover names the holon and agent role. No delivery carrying them — the skill without the plugin, or one older than align's contract 11 (Claude Code: no `hooks/rituals.mjs` in its plugin cache; OpenCode: `~/.config/opencode/plugins/verstak.js` below 3.27.0) → wire them by hand from `references/align-hooks.md`, `Initial form` (OpenCode: `references/align-harness-surfaces.md`), merged into the existing settings. Role, agent role, holon and gate may still be missing: leave them out, never invent their seqs or a gate command, never call align complete. A repeat lowers nothing that stands; spec-write waits for its slot. Then the next session starts oriented even if this one stops here. Say so to the person.

**3 · The person's role.** Registered, not proposed: the person gets their role without a question, then hears in one line what it is; renaming it by their role in this area — product owner, author, researcher — is their word, later. Then:
- search: `verstak_search(realm, q="", node_type="karta")`, any kind, and compare with `verstak_me(action="kartas")`; one already bound to them is theirs and keeps its kind — never a second;
- none bound → first the roles made for them in advance — the usual path of an invited person (an owner creates a person's role, then invites them): `verstak_search(realm, q="", node_type="karta", manifested_as="svatantra", limit=50)`, paged while it reports more, then `verstak_look` each — unbound is a card whose reading shows no user binding. Only `svatantra`: a person working in a graph is always one, so an agent's, a persona's or an outside party's role is never a candidate; a card that didn't read is not established. One whose name is the person's name fits: bind it silently, with the right to write, no "which is yours?": `verstak_update(realm, node_id=<role>, user="me", basis_version=…)`; a role made for an invitation is bound by the invitation itself, never by a guess. `me` is the account the request is signed with, not a seat, a role or the environment's name: establish that it is theirs before the write, and unknown → don't bind. Read the card again: its binding is now theirs. A refused or unclear binding or read is no ground for a second role;
- none fits → create it at once, silently, by their name — the person renames it later if they want. The kind is always `svatantra`, for the graph's owner and for an invited person alike: a person working in a graph decides for themselves whether to act; an invitation or an access level changes nothing in it. `verstak_add_karta(realm, name=<their name>, motivation="<their name>'s place in this graph", manifested_as="svatantra", user="me")`, modes per `methods/writing.md`;
- the right to write a role is not its kind: a Reader or Inquirer gets no role written through this method, and a refused write is the answer — never retried as another kind or another path. Say what they can do now; the sign stays unmet until a role is really bound.

Say what the role gives: agents address them through it, questions are posed to it, the window shows them as its holder.

**4 · Holon and repository — the standard align move.** `methods/align.md`, round 1 from step 2: the repository's holon with its `repository` attr, the agent role as its steward, the alignment case, `AGENTS.md`. The area's holon made in the window or the bot, with no `repository` attr, is reused — it takes the attr — never doubled. This move finds or creates the repository's holon by `origin`, so the binding needs no question of its own: name the binding made. The move made none → binding the repository to the area is its own question, asked again until the person binds, refuses or defers. Say what the holon is: the boundary of this repository's work in the graph, and where its agent will be found.

**5 · The agent's seat.** Readiness in the harness is an agent that can be heard, not bare tools. Once the agent role exists: the seat align took on it is it; otherwise, or to add `model` and `room`, one `verstak_stand(realm, karta=<agent role>, model=<model id>, room=<the person's seat address, if they gave one>)` — the method takes it itself; a harness taking the seat from `AGENTS.md` is not assumed. Tell the person where they will see the agent and how to ask it. The agent role's seat already held by another agent → tell the person which role is taken and under which name you stand yourself, if you took a seat.

**6 · The word about capabilities** — after align's hand-over, once. Roughly: "You can connect Telegram — agents can then ask you questions and send you messages when you are away from your desk. You can connect several repositories to one graph — their agents talk to each other, divide duties and integrate. You can share the graph with another person and their agents, or create an organization. None of this is required — the skills work without it." It teaches; it asks nothing of them, and nothing here becomes a required step.

Billing goes in the same word, from this text, never from the person's plan — an unknown plan still gets these conditions explained. Every skill and the whole method are free; what is metered is the agents' work in the graph — writes (a node or edge an agent creates or changes) and, separately, reads (orienting, browsing, search) — up to a monthly allowance. A paid plan raises both allowances; one pays when agents work in the graph every session and the free allowance runs short. In the interface you can connect your own model provider or pay for stronger models; skills and the method work on any model — connecting your own provider is free. Confirm it as understood with `understood`. Urging a purchase is the window's and the bot's, not this method's.

## Deferral and redo

- **Defer** ("skip") → go on to the next missing sign; the deferred step does not come back in this conversation, even when readiness names it next again. Say what stays missing and how to return ("say 'onboarding'"). Nothing half-made is left (a graph without rituals is fine; a role proposed but not accepted is not written). The next start reads the signs again and continues from the first missing one.
- **Redo** ("onboarding" again, a re-run for testing) → the same steps with the current values shown and the explanations repeated; it creates nothing that exists. Two runs on one account end with one graph, one role of the person's, one holon per repository. A rename is their word: `verstak_realm(action="update", realm, name=…)`, `verstak_update` on the node.
- **Three cases to tell apart**, each checked against the data:
  - a brand-new, empty working graph → step 2's initial rituals land in this session; waiting for a role or holon first is the failure;
  - an existing mind, empty or partial → reused and completed as step 0 says, never doubled, not counted as filled;
  - a second entry — a redo or a later start → the existing graph and the person's bound role are shown and kept; existing runtime roles are kept too, nothing is recreated.
- **Finishing these steps ends onboarding**, not a lock on ordinary work — that was never closed (`Steps are offers, not conditions`); it does not declare every sign met. The subject-node rows stay offers on the person's next task.
